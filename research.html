Research interests

Strongly correlated and topological quantum matter

I use quantum field theory and many-body physics to study emergent particles, unconventional metals, and phases that lie beyond weak-coupling descriptions.

01

Anyonic quantum matter

Fractional quantum Hall and fractional Chern insulator phases support anyons. On a lattice, these anyons can disperse and, once doped, form metals, superconductors, and new insulating states of their own. I study finite-density anyon fluids, disorder-driven anyon delocalization, and phase transitions out of topological order, with an emphasis on experimentally testable consequences in moiré materials.

Anyon delocalization Doping FQAH states

02

Quantum critical metals

I study metallic quantum critical points where strong fluctuations destroy quasiparticles and conventional Fermi-liquid theory fails. My work combines controlled expansions, transport theory, emergent symmetries, and anomalies to identify universal observables and exact constraints that survive even at strong coupling.

Controlled transport expansion Anomaly constraints

03

Topological and unconventional superconductivity

Fractionalization opens routes to superconductors that are qualitatively different from weak-coupling BCS states: chiral topological superconductors, higher-charge condensates, gapless topological superconductors, and phases with Majorana or parafermion defects. I am interested in both their field-theoretic structure and concrete ways to control and detect them.

Charge-4e superconductivity

04

Other directions

My broader work includes self-dual gauge-theory criticality, localization and quantum dynamics, entanglement growth, and connections between condensed matter and quantum information. These directions share a common goal: extracting sharp structure from strongly interacting quantum systems without relying on quasiparticles.
